## Mission

To establish and lead a standardized, technology-driven and financially efficient Group Supply Chain system covering inventory, materials planning, warehousing, logistics, distribution, supplier coordination and working-capital optimization across all Akij Resource businesses.

## Key Responsibilities

### A. Inventory Management
- Develop Group inventory strategy, policy, SOP and governance.
- Control inventory value, DOH, turnover and working capital.
- Ensure optimum stock availability across 300+ warehouses.
- Reduce excess, slow-moving, obsolete and dead inventory.
- Establish ABC/XYZ, FSN, criticality and aging-based inventory management.
- Lead cycle counting, physical verification and reconciliation.
- Control inventory loss, damage, leakage, shrinkage and pilferage.
- Establish inter-SBU inventory transfer and liquidation mechanisms.

### B. Materials Management
- Establish Group-wide demand planning and material requirement planning.
- Align demand, supply, procurement, production and inventory.
- Monitor RM, PM, spare parts, consumables and finished goods.
- Develop material forecasting and replenishment standards.
- Establish safety stock, reorder point, MOQ and lead-time parameters.
- Ensure critical-material availability without unnecessary overstock.

### C. Logistics & Distribution
- Establish Group logistics and distribution standards.
- Optimize primary and secondary transportation.
- Control transportation cost per MT/unit/order/km.
- Improve OTIF and customer order fulfillment.
- Optimize vehicle utilization, route planning and load consolidation.
- Establish logistics vendor performance management.
- Monitor delivery, POD, challan and shipment compliance.
- Develop centralized logistics visibility.

### D. Warehouse Management
- Standardize warehouse layout, capacity utilization, storage and handling.
- Establish warehouse productivity and safety standards.
- Optimize rented vs owned warehouse capacity.
- Control warehouse operating cost.
- Ensure system stock and physical stock accuracy.
- Develop regional warehouse/network strategy.

### E. Supplier Management
- Develop supplier performance framework for 600+ suppliers.
- Monitor OTIF, quality, lead time, price, capacity and service.
- Establish supplier segmentation and risk management.
- Develop alternate/backup supplier strategy for critical materials.

### F. Governance & Compliance
- Establish Group Inventory, Materials, Warehouse and Logistics policies.
- Ensure ERP/WMS transaction discipline.
- Standardize processes across all 52 businesses.
- Establish audit, compliance and accountability mechanisms.
- Ensure segregation of duties and authorization controls.

## 4. Operational Skills Required

At this level, operational skill means the ability to control the system through people, data and governance, rather than personally executing transactions.

- **Inventory:** ABC/XYZ analysis, FSN analysis, safety stock, reorder point, EOQ/MOQ, inventory aging, inventory optimization, cycle counting, stock reconciliation, inventory valuation
- **Materials Planning:** Demand forecasting, MRP, S&OP, supply planning, capacity planning, lead-time management, material criticality
- **Warehouse:** Warehouse design, space optimization, WMS, FIFO/FEFO, put-away/picking, warehouse productivity, warehouse costing
- **Logistics:** Route optimization, fleet planning, load optimization, transport costing, 3PL management, distribution planning, OTIF management
- **Supplier:** Supplier segmentation, supplier performance, lead-time reduction, supplier risk management, strategic sourcing coordination
- **Technology:** ERP, WMS, BI Dashboard, Power BI, Advanced Excel, data analytics, automation, AI-based forecasting and inventory intelligence

## 6. 52 Multidimensional Businesses

Businesses have different characteristics — manufacturing, trading, distribution, project, agricultural, consumer, logistics, technology. One identical inventory target cannot work for every SBU. Use a **Group Standard + SBU-specific parameter model**.

**Group Standard:** Stock accuracy >= 98-99%, SOP compliance = 100%, ERP transaction compliance = 100%, cycle count compliance = 100%, OTIF >= Group target, inventory aging control, zero unauthorized/manual delivery.

**SBU-Specific:** DOH target, safety stock, inventory turnover, lead time, warehouse utilization, service level, forecast accuracy.

Standardization without destroying business flexibility.

## 7. Major Decision Points

**Inventory:** 1. What inventory do we have? 2. Where is it? 3. Why are we holding it? 4. Is it required? 5. How much cash is locked? 6. What can be reduced/released?

**Materials:** 7. What material will become critical? 8. When will it be required? 9. Can supply meet the requirement? 10. What is the risk if supply fails?

**Warehouse:** 11. Which warehouse is overloaded? 12. Which has excess capacity? 13. Should we rent, consolidate or close a warehouse?

**Logistics:** 14. What is causing delivery failure? 15. Where is transportation cost increasing? 16. Which route/vendor/vehicle is inefficient?

**Supplier:** 17. Which supplier creates the highest operational risk? 18. Where do we need alternate suppliers?

**Financial:** 19. How much working capital can we release? 20. What is the financial impact of every inventory decision?

## 8. Inventory + Materials + Logistics = One Integrated Process

```
Demand -> Forecast -> Material Requirement -> Supply Planning -> Supplier
  -> Inbound Logistics -> Warehouse -> Inventory -> Production / SBU Requirement
  -> Distribution Planning -> Transportation -> Customer
```

Management philosophy: **Right Material -> Right Quantity -> Right Location -> Right Time -> Right Cost -> Right Customer**

## 9. Recommended Group Operating Model

- **Level 1 – Strategy:** Group Supply Chain Strategy (working capital, inventory, network, logistics, supplier strategy, digital transformation)
- **Level 2 – Planning:** Demand -> Supply -> Materials -> Inventory -> Distribution
- **Level 3 – Execution:** Procurement -> Warehouse -> Transport -> Distribution
- **Level 4 – Control:** ERP -> WMS -> BI -> Audit -> KPI -> Exception Management

## 10. Supply Chain Control Tower Role

Move from "Inventory Head who controls stock" to "Group Supply Chain Leader who controls the flow of material, inventory, logistics, cash and service."

The ultimate dashboard answers five executive questions:

1. **CASH** — How much working capital is locked in inventory?
2. **AVAILABILITY** — Can we supply the business/customer requirements?
3. **EFFICIENCY** — Are warehouses, people, suppliers and transportation operating efficiently?
4. **RISK** — Where will the next supply/inventory/logistics problem occur?
5. **ACTION** — What decision is required from me today?
